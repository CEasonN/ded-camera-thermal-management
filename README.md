# CCD Camera Thermal Management for Directed Energy Deposition

An experimental thermal-management and mechanical-design project conducted in the Advanced Research for Manufacturing Systems Laboratory (ARMS Lab) at the University of California, Davis.

The project investigates overheating of a CCD height-monitoring camera installed near the deposition region of a DMG MORI LASERTEC 65 directed energy deposition system.

## Project Motivation

The CCD camera is used for in-situ height monitoring during directed energy deposition. During extended operation, heat generated by the deposition process can accumulate in the camera, protective shield, and mounting assembly, potentially causing image distortion and reducing measurement reliability.

This project was developed to characterize the camera’s thermal response, identify critical heating locations, and support the design of a practical thermal-management solution.

## Objectives

* Characterize temperature evolution during directed energy deposition
* Identify the camera components most susceptible to overheating
* Evaluate thermal behavior during deposition and cooldown
* Estimate camera temperature during longer deposition sequences
* Improve the mechanical mounting and thermal-management design
* Support reliable in-situ imaging during additive manufacturing

## Experimental Approach

Thermocouples were installed at six locations around the monitoring system:

* Camera lens
* Camera interior
* Camera rear surface
* Camera top surface
* Protective shield
* Deposition nozzle

Temperature data were collected during multiple deposition stages, including sequential 10-layer runs, a continuous 50-layer deposition, and the subsequent cooldown period.

The multi-channel measurements were used to compare local heating rates and identify how heat propagated through the camera and mounting assembly.

## Data Analysis

MATLAB was used to:

* Import multi-channel thermocouple measurements
* Generate temperature–time curves for each sensor location
* Compare consecutive deposition stages
* Analyze the 50-layer deposition and cooldown response
* Apply polynomial regression to selected temperature profiles
* Extrapolate camera-lens temperature for a longer 200-layer deposition scenario

The analysis provided an estimate of the camera’s thermal exposure during extended operation and helped identify components requiring improved thermal protection.

## Mechanical Design

SolidWorks was used to design and evaluate updated mounting components, including:

* Camera mounting brackets
* L-block components
* Spacer components
* Assembly configurations
* Manufacturing drawings

The design work focused on maintaining camera alignment and mechanical stability while supporting improved thermal management and reliable process monitoring.

## Running the MATLAB Analysis

1. Clone or download the repository.
2. Open MATLAB and navigate to the repository directory.
3. Confirm that the experimental CSV files referenced by `CCDCameraExperiment.m` are available.
4. Update the file paths or data ranges if necessary.
5. Run `CCDCameraExperiment.m`.
6. Review the generated temperature–time plots, polynomial fits, and extended-deposition temperature prediction.
